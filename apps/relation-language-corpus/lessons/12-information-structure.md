# Lesson 12: Topic, Focus, Contrast, Repair

A sentence does more than name an event.
It also tells the listener how to place the event in the conversation.

RelConLang marks this with four small particles.

```text
ma = topic, already-open matter
fi = focus, new contribution
ko = contrast, chosen against alternatives
ri = repair, replacement term
```

## Topic

`ma` follows the topic.

```text
mi ma ran kelu.
I TOP relate relation
"As for me, I relate the relation."
```

The subject is still `mi`, and `ma` adds a topic arc.

```text
ran --nsubj--> mi
ran --topic--> mi
mi --mark--> ma
```

## Focus

`fi` comes before the focused term.

```text
mi ran fi sava.
I relate FOC witness
"I relate the witness."
```

This is useful when a prior question made the object the important new answer.

```text
ku tu ran kano?
Q you relate capability
"Do you relate the capability?"

se mi ran fi sava.
ANS I relate FOC witness
"No / answer: I relate the witness."
```

## Contrast

`ko` marks one live alternative against another.

```text
ko tu kan kano?
CONTR you can capability
"Can you, rather than another candidate, enact the capability?"
```

## Repair

`ri` marks a replacement term.

```text
mi ran ri hena.
I relate REPAIR coordination
"I relate coordination as the replacement term."
```

This is how an agent begins to repair without erasing the old utterance.
The old claim can remain witnessed; the new term is marked as replacement.

## With Explicit Roles

Information structure can stack with role receipts.

```text
mi ga ran do fi sava.
I A relate P FOC witness
"I relate the witness, with explicit object role and object focus."
```

The role particle `do` says that `sava` is the object.
The focus particle `fi` says that `sava` is the new contribution.
